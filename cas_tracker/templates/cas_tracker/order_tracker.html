How to read this book (why buy prices sit above sell prices)

A closing auction is not a continuous market. Nothing executes until 15:30, when the exchange picks the single price that clears the most volume. Until then the book legitimately looks “crossed” — in a continuous market that would be an error; here it is the normal state.

  • Orders at the band edges are market orders. NSE caps auction prices at ±3% of the reference price. A buy displayed at the upper band means “I’ll pay up to the maximum allowed”; a sell at the lower band means “I’ll accept down to the minimum allowed.” Both are aggressive orders that will execute — at the equilibrium price, never at the band price they’re shown at.
  • At-auction (ATO) orders carry no price at all and so never appear on the ladder. They’re counted in the book totals above, and they’re usually a large share of the book.
  • The ladder is a small slice of the book. Neither feed publishes the full order book — NSE gives 4 price levels a side, Kite 5, and this page merges both. For a liquid stock mid-auction the visible levels routinely sum to under a tenth of the real book, so always read the whole book totals above, never the sum of the rows below.
  • “Would match at IEP” is not the book size. It’s the volume that actually crosses at the current equilibrium price. Buy orders priced below it and sell orders priced above it simply don’t participate, so matched volume is always far smaller than total orders.

Data is only recorded during the 15:14–15:40 IST window, and NSE only populates the ladder during order collection (roughly 15:20–15:30) — outside that it is empty by design.
